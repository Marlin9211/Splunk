How to work through the application
Read the mission context first. For every activity, examine the evidence before choosing an action. Do not choose an answer only because it contains familiar Splunk terminology. Summit Renewable Energy // Incident SR-204

The dashboard is technically “working.” The answer is not.

Situation: Summit Renewable Energy’s customer energy-services platform is processing a high volume of purchase traffic while operations investigates inconsistent transaction behavior. The data is available in Splunk, but the current search is broad: it carries unnecessary fields, repeats client activity, mixes user-name casing, and presents results in an order that makes review difficult.

Operational request

Leadership needs a compact, explainable view of purchase activity showing the client IP, user, status, and sale price. The goal is not simply to reduce the number of rows. The team must understand what the search retrieved, what each command changed, what evidence was removed or retained, and why the final result can be trusted.

Your role: Search Intelligence Analyst. You will move through three connected responsibilities. As the Operator, you will construct and apply precise searches. As the Detective, you will investigate why reasonable-looking searches can produce different evidence. As the Judge, you will evaluate complete search strategies and defend the one whose behavior best satisfies the operational requirements.

Mission boundary: Do not treat a smaller result set as automatically better. Every change must have an explainable purpose tied to retrieval precision, field visibility, duplicate handling, ordering, transformation, or presentation.

Final Debrief & Reflection

The search is a chain of decisions.

Retrieve precisely

Fields are searchable name/value pairs. Field expressions usually provide more precise searches than relying only on keywords or quoted phrases.

Transform intentionally

Each pipeline command receives the prior command's output. Order can change the evidence that survives.

Present responsibly

A smaller table is useful only when you can explain what was filtered, removed, transformed, or renamed.
